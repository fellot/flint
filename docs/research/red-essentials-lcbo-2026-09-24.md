# Red cellar essentials: LCBO buying shortlist

Researched September 24, 2026. Based on Felipe’s Supabase export from the same day: 36 active bottles and 27 personal journal entries, all scored. Recommendations favour dry wines, unfamiliar styles and meaningful additions; there is no budget ceiling.

## The actual gaps

Four red essentials are absent from both the active cellar and journal: **Right Bank Bordeaux, Napa Cabernet Sauvignon, Chilean Carménère and Etna Rosso**. A **Malbec-led Mendoza wine** is an additional stylistic gap. Northern Rhône Syrah is a **restock**, already explored and highly rated.

The website currently counts Gran Enemigo Gualtallary toward Mendoza Malbec because its grape field mentions Malbec. The 2019 is **85% Cabernet Franc and 15% Malbec**, so it does not establish a Malbec-led benchmark. ## Purchase shortlist

Prices are displayed Canadian dollars per 750 mL bottle. **Only the As Bravas stock count below was confirmed on the live Cellar Collection ordering table.** The other links are verified product listings with stock unconfirmed; check your selected store or delivery availability before planning a purchase. A listing or an “Add to Cart” label alone is not proof of stock.

### 1. Etna Rosso — Benanti Contrada Dafara Galluzzo 2022

**C$94 · LCBO 30116 · stock unconfirmed.**

My first exploration pick: Nerello Mascalese offers a new grape and origin while retaining the aromatic detail and structure that make your Italian favourites interesting. LCBO describes a medium-bodied wine with red fruit, citrus peel and firm, fine tannins; listed sugar is 3 g/L. Its published note suggests drinking now or holding into the early 2030s.

[Benanti at LCBO](https://www.lcbo.com/en/benanti-contrada-dafara-galluzzo-30116)

The less expensive Tornatore Pietrarizzo 2022 appeared in the search index at C$40.95, but its direct page returned “no longer here” during the live check. It is therefore excluded from the actionable shortlist. The Cellar Collection’s available-online search returned no Etna products during this check; this does not establish that all LCBO stores are out of Etna wine.

### 2. Carménère — Clos de Luz Massal 1945 2023

**C$24.95 · LCBO 11861 · stock unconfirmed.**

A useful introduction to the grape’s herbal, peppery personality, distinct from your Chilean Cabernet, Syrah and Carignan. The old-vine Cachapoal wine has red fruit and firm tannins; LCBO lists 2 g/L sugar and a published drinking horizon through 2033+. It earns its place through stylistic relevance, despite its modest price.

[Clos de Luz at LCBO](https://www.lcbo.com/en/clos-de-luz-massal-1945-carmen-rre-2016-11861)

Alternative: **Santa Rita Floresta Carménère 2023**, LCBO 34733, indexed at **C$23.25 clearance** (regular C$27.95), stock unconfirmed. Concrete ageing and an explicitly fresh, herbal expression make this particularly appealing if you want less oak influence. [Floresta at LCBO](https://www.lcbo.com/en/santa-rita-floresta-carmenere-34733).

### 3. Right Bank Bordeaux — Château Canon-la-Gaffelière 2018

**C$200 · LCBO 45222 · stock unconfirmed.**

Saint-Émilion supplies the geographic and blending reference missing beside your Pauillac and Haut-Médoc. This bottle also brings some age to a cellar containing many young wines. LCBO’s cited drinking window is **2026–2043**; its note highlights floral, savoury and dark-fruit character, with 3 g/L listed sugar. Being inside a drinking window does not establish an exact peak year.

[Canon-la-Gaffelière at LCBO](https://www.lcbo.com/en/chateau-canon-la-gaffeliere-45222)

A lower-priced long-term option is **Château Laroque 2023**, listed at **C$55**, with a cited **2033–2053** window. Treat it as a bottle for patience rather than the immediate tasting comparison. Stock/order terms need checking. [Laroque at LCBO](https://www.lcbo.com/en/chateau-laroque-2023-40496).

### 4. Mendoza Malbec — El Enemigo As Bravas 2017

**C$150 · LCBO 50299 · September Cellar Collection showed 20 bottles available.**

The most adventurous Cellar Collection find: **95% Malbec, 5% Sémillon**, with extended ageing in large used oak casks. The published note describes restrained fruit, freshness and developed texture, with a **2026–2042** drinking window and an extra-dry classification. It gives you a Malbec-led companion to Gran Enemigo without repeating that wine’s Cabernet Franc focus.

[September Cellar Collection — page 1, item 50299](https://www.vintagesshoponline.com/vintages/Public/OrderProgramProducts.aspx?programId=1332&lang=en)

The collection calls it “El Enemigo As Bravas Gualtallary Malbec 2017”; the embedded critic note names El Cepillo. The recommendation uses Mendoza/Uco Valley as the broad origin and does not resolve that subregion inconsistency.

For a more conventional, inexpensive varietal reference, **Catena Malbec**, **C$22.95**, LCBO 478727, is also a sensible choice; the listing does not establish the bottle’s current vintage. Stock unconfirmed. [Catena at LCBO](https://www.lcbo.com/en/catena-malbec-478727).

The September collection also listed **Catena Zapata Argentino Malbec 2023**, C$129, item 38228, but showed **zero bottles available**. It is a watchlist option, not confirmed purchasable stock.

### 5. Napa Cabernet — pending LCBO availability

This gap cannot currently be filled through LCBO: its current notice says U.S. products are unavailable online and in stores until further notice. [LCBO status](https://www.lcbo.com/content/lcbo/en/corporate-pages/lcbo-response-to-u-s--tariffs--q-a.html).

**Faust Cabernet Sauvignon 2021**, LCBO 238261, is a useful future reference, listed at C$74.95 but explicitly unavailable online. [Faust listing](https://www.lcbo.com/fr/cabernet-sauvignon-faust-2018-238261). Another country’s Cabernet would not fill the Napa-origin slot. Your Casa Real already provides a substantial Cabernet option while this remains pending.

## Two optional additions

- **Restock Northern Rhône:** [J. L. Chave Sélection Offerus Saint-Joseph 2023](https://www.lcbo.com/en/j-l-chave-s-clection-offerus-saint-joseph-2018-359109), **C$50.95**, LCBO 359109, stock unconfirmed. This is a new appellation within a category you already rated 95. LCBO lists 2 g/L sugar and describes it as approachable now with development through 2033.
- **Explore traditional Rioja:** [López de Heredia Viña Tondonia Reserva 2013](https://www.lcbo.com/en/r-l-pez-de-heredia-vi-a-tondonia-reserva-2009-356337), **C$82.95**, LCBO 356337, stock unconfirmed. This adds a mature Reserva reference alongside Torre Muga. The live listing’s Tim Atkin note gives **2027–2040**; the search index carried a different review, so those windows have not been combined.

## Practical order

For new styles, prioritize **Etna → Carménère → Right Bank Bordeaux → Malbec**. Choose one bottle per gap, taste, then decide whether to deepen the category. If the aim is to keep every essential physically stocked, add a Northern Rhône bottle too. Napa remains pending, and traditional Rioja is an optional refinement.

Stock observations are a September 24 snapshot, not a reservation. No bottles were ordered, and no cellar records or website code were changed.
